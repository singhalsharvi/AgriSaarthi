# Tomato - Late Blight

## Crop
Tomato

## Disease
Late Blight

## Description
A profile of Late Blight affecting Tomato plants. This condition primarily impacts the leaves, stems, and fruits. with a severity rating of extremely high. can decimate a crop in 7-14 days and spread to neighboring farms..

## Symptoms
Large, irregular water-soaked spots on leaves that rapidly turn brown/black. A white, fuzzy fungal growth develops on the undersides of leaves in humid conditions. Fruits develop large, greasy, chocolate-brown lesions.

## Visual symptoms
Affected plants display symptoms on leaves, stems, and fruits.: Large, irregular water-soaked spots on leaves that rapidly turn brown/black. A white, fuzzy fungal growth develops on the undersides of leaves in humid conditions. Fruits develop large, greasy, chocolate-brown lesions.

## Causes
Oomycete pathogen Phytophthora infestans. Spores are windborne and travel long distances.

## Risk factors
Severity is rated extremely high. can decimate a crop in 7-14 days and spread to neighboring farms.. Key risk factors include planting in fields with a history of infection, poor sanitation, and not sanitizing farming tools.

## Environmental conditions
Cool (60-70°F), damp, cloudy, and wet weather with relative humidity above 90%.

## Prevention
Plant resistant cultivars. Avoid planting tomatoes near potatoes. Immediately destroy infected plants by bagging and disposing of them.
Drip irrigation is preferred to keep foliage dry. Avoid working in the crops during wet periods.

## Management/treatment
Apply copper fungicides weekly during cool, wet weather. In commercial settings, apply specific oomycete-targeted fungicides (e.g., chlorothalonil + mandipropamid).
