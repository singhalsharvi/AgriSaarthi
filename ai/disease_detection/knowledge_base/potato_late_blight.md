# Potato - Late Blight

## Crop
Potato

## Disease
Late Blight

## Description
A profile of Late Blight affecting Potato plants. This condition primarily impacts the leaves, stems, and tubers. with a severity rating of extremely high. can destroy entire fields within days if left untreated..

## Symptoms
Large, dark green to purple-black water-soaked lesions starting at leaf tips/margins. Under humid conditions, a white velvety mold grows on the underside of leaves. Stems turn brown/black, and tubers show dry, reddish-brown rot.

## Visual symptoms
Affected plants display symptoms on leaves, stems, and tubers.: Large, dark green to purple-black water-soaked lesions starting at leaf tips/margins. Under humid conditions, a white velvety mold grows on the underside of leaves. Stems turn brown/black, and tubers show dry, reddish-brown rot.

## Causes
Oomycete pathogen Phytophthora infestans. It is highly infectious and spreads by wind-blown spores.

## Risk factors
Severity is rated extremely high. can destroy entire fields within days if left untreated.. Key risk factors include planting in fields with a history of infection, poor sanitation, and not sanitizing farming tools.

## Environmental conditions
Cool, wet, humid weather (50-78°F, relative humidity >90%). Common in rain or heavy fog/dew.

## Prevention
Plant certified late-blight-resistant seed tubers. Monitor local disease alerts. Destroy volunteer potato plants and cull piles.
Harvest during dry weather. Allow vines to die completely 2 weeks before harvesting tubers to reduce skin contamination.

## Management/treatment
Apply preventive systemic fungicides (e.g., metalaxyl, fluopicolide) or copper fungicides at regular intervals when disease is active. Immediately remove and destroy infected plants.
