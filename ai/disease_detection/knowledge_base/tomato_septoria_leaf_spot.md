# Tomato - Septoria Leaf Spot

## Crop
Tomato

## Disease
Septoria Leaf Spot

## Description
A profile of Septoria Leaf Spot affecting Tomato plants. This condition primarily impacts the leaves and stems. with a severity rating of high. progresses upward and causes severe defoliation, leading to sunscald and stunted fruit growth..

## Symptoms
Numerous small, circular spots (1.6-3.2mm) with dark brown borders and light grey or tan centers. Tiny black specks (fruiting bodies) appear in the centers of mature spots. Starts on lower leaves.

## Visual symptoms
Affected plants display symptoms on leaves and stems.: Numerous small, circular spots (1.6-3.2mm) with dark brown borders and light grey or tan centers. Tiny black specks (fruiting bodies) appear in the centers of mature spots. Starts on lower leaves.

## Causes
Fungus Septoria lycopersici. Spores splash from soil onto lower leaves.

## Risk factors
Severity is rated high. progresses upward and causes severe defoliation, leading to sunscald and stunted fruit growth.. Key risk factors include planting in fields with a history of infection, poor sanitation, and not sanitizing farming tools.

## Environmental conditions
Warm temperatures (68-77°F), wet leaves, and frequent rainfall or overhead irrigation.

## Prevention
Apply mulch to create a barrier between soil-borne spores and foliage. Practice a 3-year crop rotation. Avoid overhead irrigation.
Keep fields free of solanaceous weeds (e.g., nightshade) which can host the pathogen.

## Management/treatment
Apply protectant fungicides (e.g., chlorothalonil, mancozeb) at 7-10 day intervals. Prune lower diseased leaves.
