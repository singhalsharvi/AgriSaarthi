# Tomato - Spider Mites (Two-spotted Spider Mite)

## Crop
Tomato

## Disease
Spider Mites (Two-spotted Spider Mite)

## Description
A profile of Spider Mites (Two-spotted Spider Mite) affecting Tomato plants. This condition primarily impacts the leaves and stems. with a severity rating of high. reduces photosynthetic capacity, leading to leaf drop and small, poor-quality fruit..

## Symptoms
Fine yellow or white speckling on the upper surface of leaves. As infestation progresses, leaves turn yellow, bronze, dry up, and drop. Fine webbing is visible on the undersides of leaves and stems.

## Visual symptoms
Affected plants display symptoms on leaves and stems.: Fine yellow or white speckling on the upper surface of leaves. As infestation progresses, leaves turn yellow, bronze, dry up, and drop. Fine webbing is visible on the undersides of leaves and stems.

## Causes
Pest Tetranychus urticae. Very small arachnids that feed by sucking cell contents.

## Risk factors
Severity is rated high. reduces photosynthetic capacity, leading to leaf drop and small, poor-quality fruit.. Key risk factors include planting in fields with a history of infection, poor sanitation, and not sanitizing farming tools.

## Environmental conditions
Hot (>80°F), dry, and dusty weather. Often worsens after broad-spectrum insecticide use kills natural predators.

## Prevention
Maintain plant health (stressed plants are more susceptible). Overhead misting can help suppress populations. Encourage natural predators like ladybugs and predatory mites.
Keep farm borders free of weeds. Wash plants with a strong stream of water to dislodge mites and dust.

## Management/treatment
Apply insecticidal soaps, horticultural oils, neem oil, or specific miticides (e.g., abamectin) targeting the undersides of leaves.
