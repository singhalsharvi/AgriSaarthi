# Tomato - Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus

## Crop
Tomato

## Disease
Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus

## Description
A profile of Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus affecting Tomato plants. This condition primarily impacts the leaves, stems, and flowers. with a severity rating of very high. if plants are infected early, they will produce zero marketable fruit..

## Symptoms
Severe stunting of new growth. Leaves cup upward and roll inward, showing pale green or yellow margins (chlorosis). Flowers fail to set fruit or drop off, resulting in drastic yield loss.

## Visual symptoms
Affected plants display symptoms on leaves, stems, and flowers.: Severe stunting of new growth. Leaves cup upward and roll inward, showing pale green or yellow margins (chlorosis). Flowers fail to set fruit or drop off, resulting in drastic yield loss.

## Causes
Begomovirus (TYLCV), transmitted exclusively by the silverleaf whitefly (Bemisia tabaci). It is not transmitted mechanically or through seeds.

## Risk factors
Severity is rated very high. if plants are infected early, they will produce zero marketable fruit.. Key risk factors include planting in fields with a history of infection, poor sanitation, and not sanitizing farming tools.

## Environmental conditions
Warm, dry climates that encourage whitefly reproduction and migration.

## Prevention
Use virus-resistant cultivars. Apply row covers to protect young seedlings. Manage whitefly populations using yellow sticky traps and reflective mulches.
Maintain a 2-month host-free period for whiteflies/tomatoes between seasons to break the transmission cycle.

## Management/treatment
No cure exists for the virus once infected. Immediately remove and destroy infected plants. Spray whiteflies with neem oil, insecticidal soap, or systemic insecticides.
